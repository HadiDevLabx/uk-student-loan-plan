Which student loan plan am I on?

The plan decides your threshold, and the thresholds differ by nearly £9,000 — so the same salary repays very different amounts depending on which one you are. Three questions.

1 · Where were you living when you applied?
2 · When did your course start?
3 · What kind of course?

The five plans

PlanWhoThresholdRate
Plan 1England or Wales before Sept 2012 · Northern Ireland, any year——
Plan 2England or Wales, Sept 2012 to July 2023 · Wales from Aug 2023 too——
Plan 4Scotland — loans through SAAS——
Plan 5England only, courses starting on or after 1 Aug 2023——
PostgraduateEngland or Wales master's and doctoral loans——

Plan 5 has the lowest threshold of the lot. That is counter-intuitive — it is the newest plan, and it starts taking money earlier than Plan 2 does. On the same salary a Plan 5 borrower repays more each month than a Plan 2 borrower, from the first pound over the line.

A postgraduate loan does not replace your undergraduate one. If you have both, both are deducted, each against its own threshold. That catches people who assume the later loan supersedes the earlier one.

Things the three questions cannot see

Where you were living when you applied is what counts, not where you studied — a Scottish student at an English university is normally Plan 4 — and Scottish income tax differs too, which the Scottish income tax calculator covers. Northern Ireland has stayed on Plan 1 throughout. And Wales did not follow England onto Plan 5: Welsh students starting after August 2023 remain Plan 2.

If you have loans from more than one period you can be on more than one plan at once, and each is collected separately. The income tax calculator shows tax, NI and both loans on one salary.

What it costs on your salary

This page tells you the plan. The student loan repayment calculator puts a salary against it and shows the monthly deduction, and the take-home pay calculator shows it alongside tax, National Insurance and pension so you see the real net figure.

If you are near a threshold, the pay rise calculator is worth a look — crossing a student loan threshold adds 9% to your marginal rate on top of tax and NI, which is why some raises feel smaller than they are. The bonus tax calculator and the overtime tax calculator both show the same squeeze on one-off payments.

Thresholds here are read live from uk-tax-rates, a free CC BY 4.0 dataset. Related: what changed this tax year and the tax code decoder.

A guide, not a determination. The Student Loans Company and your online repayment account are authoritative for your own loan. Edge cases exist — transferred courses, EU students, pre-1998 mortgage-style loans — that three questions cannot cover.
